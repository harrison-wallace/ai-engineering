## 1. Marketing – Create "Shareable Cool"
**Core Principle**
No one cares about you or your product when they wake up. You must create something so surprising, funny, useful, relatable, or "can you believe this?" that they stop scrolling and actively show it to friends/colleagues.

- Target communities / first places to post:

**Quick Checklist**
- [ ] Would I personally forward this to a friend in the niche?
- [ ] Zero chance it feels like a "new feature announcement"?
- [ ] Designed to be seen by people who have never heard of us?

**Common Pitfalls**
- Boring blog posts, feature drops, influencer "reviews"
- Asking AI to brainstorm the hook (it always ends up corny)

## 2. Aha Moment – Ruthlessly Simplify to One Singular Moment
**Core Principle**
There must be one exact moment where the user thinks "Holy shit, this is why this exists." Everything else is noise. Get them there in <60 seconds or you lose them.

**For this project**
- Exact Aha moment defined (one sentence):
- Target time from first click to Aha:

**Quick Checklist**
- [ ] Onboarding = maximum 2–3 taps before Aha
- [ ] No company-size, title, or "how many employees?" questions before value
- [ ] Tested with 5+ real target users (time recorded)
- [ ] Every extra screen/step justified with data or removed

**Common Pitfalls**
- Wanting users to see "all the good features" too early
- "Kill your darlings" resistance

## 3. Retention – Build Primitives First, Simple UX on Top
**Core Principle**
Build powerful, flexible primitives under the hood first. Then wrap the simplest possible experience for 99% of users. Never build something people outgrow.

**Quick Checklist**
- [ ] Data schema designed for future complexity today
- [ ] Free / starter users never feel "this is a toy"
- [ ] Power users can flex the product without leaving
- [ ] No new product needed when user grows

**Common Pitfalls**
- Building "simple but incapable" (Apple fallacy)
- Adding features directly instead of extending primitives
